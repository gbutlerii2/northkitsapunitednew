North Kitsap United (NKU) is a collaborative initiative created to meet the growing need for quality sports fields and active recreation facilities

PAGE 2: CONCEPT - CONTEXT

The Raydient property is comprised of approximately 400+ acres on SR 307 adjacent to the Port Gamble Forest Heritage Park.

The property was originally part of a corporate tree farm originated in the 1800s that included the town of Port Gamble. Most of that holding now 
comprises the Port Gamble Forest Heritage Park. The next timber harvest will be its fourth rotation.

In 2007, then-property owner Pope Resources announced that it was on track to sell all of its North Kitsap lands. Local conditions—chiefly 
suburbanization—were changing rapidly, and the company realized the community would soon be intolerant of commercial timberland management. From 
2007 to 2017 Pope subsidiary Olympic Property Group worked extensively with the community and a host of other organizations to raise money and 
transfer ownership of the property to Kitsap County. This effort created the “String of Pearls," a vast system of public trails and open spaces.

In 2020, Rayonier acquired Pope Resources. Rayonier will continue Pope’s plan of selling all its ownership in North Kitsap County in the years 
ahead. All of Raydient's local timberlands were subdivided into 20-acre lots more than 15-years ago.

PAGE 5: PLANNING TEAM – KINGSTON-NORTH KITSAP ROTARY CLUB

The Kingston-North Kitsap Rotary Club is a non-profit service club focused on community service and engagement in the North Kitsap area (Kingston, Hansville, Eglon, Indianola, Suquamish and unincorporated areas of Poulsbo). Over the years, the club has surveyed members of the Kingston and greater North Kitsap communities to identify the most pressing needs and focused their efforts on filling those needs. Examples include trail markers in the Heritage Park, lights at the Kingston High School football and track & field, an improved library and community center (Village Green Community Center, Park & Picnic Pavilion), and a way to better communicate on-going information (electronic reader board).
 
Over the past few years, it has come to their attention that there is a lack of necessary sports and recreation facilities available for the youth in North Kitsap. A project to fulfil this need was officially announced at their February 2023 fundraising auction and immediately gained significant community support. Soon afterwards, they were made aware of the opportunity to work on a larger vision in collaboration with Raydient and YMCA that has the potential to better fill these sports and recreation needs.
 
Rotary’s role in this vision is focusing on the creation of a multi-generational, multi-use sports and recreation complex that fulfils the community’s need and complements existing facilities in North Kitsap. The vision is for this complex to consist of indoor and outdoor facilities that provide a wide range of sports and recreation opportunities for all ages – as well as health and wellness amenities. The Kingston-North Kitsap Rotary Club plans on leading the fundraising efforts, raising awareness, and facilitating partnerships for this sports and recreation complex on behalf of the North Kitsap community.


For more information about the Kingston-North Kitsap Rotary Club and their role in this project, please visit their website: www.rotaryknk.org .

PROJECT HISTORY:

On June 27th, 2023, Raydient Places + Properties, the YMCA of Pierce and Kitsap Counties, and the Rotary Club of Kingston-North Kitsap announced to 
the community that they were working together to explore an idea.

The concept originally entailed Raydient rezoning the centrally-located 400 acres it owns on SR307 resulting in an increase in the number of 
single-family residential lots—from 20 to 80—and use “lot clustering” to create 100 to 200 acres of open space. Within a portion of the open space, 
the goal would then be to find a home for a new YMCA and a sports/recreation complex. The complex would be central to all North Kitsap Communities 
and adjacent to the Port Gamble Forest Heritage Park, thus “uniting” both our communities and a multitude of healthy activities. The rezone would 
have allowed Raydient to donate the land to the YMCA and Rotary Club. However, on June 23, 2025, the Kitsap County Commissioners voted 2-to-1 to 
not allow the rezone.

Because of this, the Kingston-North Kitsap Rotary Club and the YMCA are actively engaged with another non-profit organization – among other partners
 – to raise enough funds to buy the land and continue the project and vision without Raydient and any concepts having to do with housing. The 
 decision to press forward spawned on December 16, 2025. More than 60-Government and Non-Profit Organizations and 3,750+ households in North Kitsap 
 have publicly endorsed this project and the Rotary is using the Kitsap Great Give (Kitsap Foundation | Kitsap Great Give| Kitsap Community Foundation)
  as its first active fundraising endeavor to encourage people to donate to purchase the land.

 

The list of FAQ’s below addresses many of the questions and concerns raised so far. We intend to update it regularly, so please check back from 
time-to-time.

 

What is North Kitsap United (NKU)?
North Kitsap United (NKU) is a collaborative initiative created to meet the growing need for quality sports fields and active recreation facilities 
in North Kitsap County — a region where youth and families currently face limited and overcrowded play spaces and little to no options for active 
recreational activities. Limited services and amenities are also prevalent in health care and wellness services and childcare – other key elements 
incorporated into NKU’s project.

 

As our community grows, so does the demand for accessible places where kids can play, practice, compete, and stay healthy. This NKU project brings 
together local partners and leaders to deliver multi-generational recreation space that serves ALL ages.

 

What is the vision for this project?
We envision a vibrant sports and active recreation complex on centrally-located land made possible through community support, strong partnerships, and strategic conservation. The complex will offer:

Multiple sports fields for soccer, baseball/softball, football, lacrosse, and more
Space for numerous community leagues, practices, and tournamentsYear-round opportunities for play, fitness and active recreation, and extended family activities
Connections to and an extension of existing trails and public green spaces
Promotes health, teamwork, leadership, and community pride
A YMCA
 

This project will strengthen the youth sports leagues of today, support local clubs for continued advancement, and foster active lifestyles for all 
ages and for generations to come.

 

What about the Rezone?
Following the County Commissioners’ decision of ‘No’ on the rezone application on June 23, 2025, the project has shifted direction. Raydient is now 
working toward a land purchase agreement with the Kingston-North Kitsap Rotary Club and another non-profit conservation organization for the 400+ 
acres off Bond Road. The vision continues to include a YMCA and a sports and recreation campus, while the residential housing component is no longer 
part of the project.

 

Why is Kingston-North Kitsap Rotary Club championing this project?
Kingston–North Kitsap Rotary recognized a growing, ongoing need for additional sports fields and recreational opportunities in North Kitsap dating 
back to 2016. Our members are parents, athletes, coaches, fans, and neighbors—people who see firsthand how limited access to quality facilities 
affects kids, families, and teams truly is.

 

For years, our club has been committed to the idea of a welcoming, accessible place where anyone – no matter their age or affiliation to any 
organization – can reserve a field or court and practice or play at a well-maintained, high-quality venue. Families want a better youth sports 
experience—one that offers adequate fields, reasonable practice times, and the ability to stay close to home rather than traveling across Kitsap 
County and beyond - for practices, games and tournaments.

 

Why collaborate with a Non-Profit Conservancy, Raydient and YMCA?
The Kingston-North Kitsap Rotary Club views this as a unique opportunity to bring a sports and recreation complex and 300+ more acres of forest 
preservation, trails and purposeful animal wildlife corridors to the North Kitsap community – something the community has asked about for years and 
a need that has been discussed and identified dating back to 1995. Rotary has been working on and researching this project since 2017 and this land 
acquisition opportunity offers a location and organizations to collaborate with on a mutual vision. Each of the four organizations brings different 
expertise to this project and broadens their respective abilities to create a facility that will fulfill the current and future needs of North Kitsap 
and benefit the community for years to come.  Further, the location of the Raydient land is centrally located to ALL our North Kitsap communities, 
providing a greater benefit.

 

How will this collaboration work?
Kingston-North Kitsap Rotary is excited about working with a multitude of government, non-profit organizations – alongside the YMCA to use land 
currently held by Raydient to build a sports and recreation complex and extend the Port Gamble Heritage Park for the North Kitsap community.  
Each organization will be responsible for different parts of the larger project and its costs, with our focus being on the funding and construction 
of a wide range of outdoor facilities - as well as indoor facilities not covered by the YMCA complex vision. There will be several phases to the 
project. Phase One is the Land Acquisition – where we are today. It is our hope to have the funds raised and have the land in our possession to start 
the complex construction by the end of 2027. The YMCA is several years away from the start of their project construction.

 

Why partner with YMCA?
We believe there is powerful synergy in pairing a YMCA with a public sports and recreation campus. Together, they create a hub for connection, 
wellness, and opportunity. The YMCA brings a wide range of programs that help meet currently unmet needs in North Kitsap, including childcare, 
health and wellness services, and youth and family support. North Kitsap teens lack safe and available gathering opportunities. The Y offers teen 
programs at no cost as well as elderly services that otherwise are only available in the Greater Seattle Areas. 

 

What is the Kingston–North Kitsap Rotary Club’s role in this effort?
Kingston–North Kitsap Rotary’s role is to help move this community vision forward by building awareness, leading fundraising efforts, and convening partners in support of a future sports and recreation complex that serves North Kitsap.

 

Rotary will not be the builder or operator of the facility. Rather, we see our role as a catalyst and connector—providing early momentum, community support, and resources to help make the project possible. This mirrors our involvement with the Village Green, where Rotarians helped champion the idea and raise funds, while long-term ownership and management were handled by others. We anticipate a similar role for Rotary as this project continues to take shape.

 

Who will operate and maintain this facility?
As with the Village Green, the Kingston–North Kitsap Rotary Club does not intend to build, own, operate, or maintain the facility. The long-term vision is for the project to be transferred to a public agency, government entity, or non-profit organization (like the YMCA) with the expertise and capacity to operate and maintain it sustainably.

 

Rotary’s role is to help advance the project to a point where long-term stewardship is possible. We are actively in conversation with potential owner-operators and will continue working to identify the best long-term solution to ensure the facility is well-managed, accessible, and maintained for the benefit of the North Kitsap community.

 

What is the need for a sports complex in North Kitsap?
There is a well-documented shortage of sports and recreation facilities throughout Kitsap County, and it is most acute in North Kitsap. The “North Kitsap Recreation Facility Feasibility Study” conducted by Berk & Associates in December 2024 found that North Kitsap has roughly half the number of athletic fields expected for a community of its size, based on national benchmarks. Most existing fields (80+%) are owned by the North Kitsap School District, which significantly limits public access.

 

As a result, youth sports leagues and community groups face chronic shortages, overcrowding, limited practice times, and safety concerns related to field conditions and overuse. Even with planned improvements, current and projected demand far exceeds available capacity.

 

This gap will continue to widen as North Kitsap grows. County planning anticipates significant population increases in the coming decades, with North Kitsap absorbing a disproportionate share of that growth. Despite decades of community discussion and past efforts to develop recreational facilities (Kitsap County in 2002), the need has never been fully met. The study confirms that North Kitsap lacks both the quantity and diversity of facilities needed to serve residents equitably, now and in the future.

 

11.)                             What can we do to fix current fields in the meantime?

Kingston–North Kitsap Rotary has a long history of supporting and improving existing sports and recreation facilities. The club has worked with the North Kitsap School District (NKSD) and Kingston Youth Sports Association (KYSA) to help improve the fields and facilities at Kingston Middle School, and previously fundraised for field lighting at Kingston High School (2008). Rotary members also support annually the maintenance of local trails and open spaces.

 

This proposed sports and recreation complex is intended to complement—not replace—existing facilities by relieving pressure on overused fields, expanding access, and helping North Kitsap meet both current and future community needs. Local nonprofit organizations and schools are encouraged to apply for a club grant: http://rotaryknk.org/documents/en-ca/887f63de-645c-48e9-84e8-127b6269a670/1

Won’t this sports complex be competing with the proposed Poulsbo Event & Recreation Center (PERC)?
No. Kingston–North Kitsap Rotary does not intend to build competing infrastructure. The goal is to complement existing and planned facilities by partnering with PERC and other organizations to meet the broader recreational needs of North Kitsap. We commend them on their efforts to help bridge our community’s gap and need with this project and are excited to see the completion of it soon.

 

Rotary is committed to working collaboratively with the City of Poulsbo and other community partners to ensure that facilities are coordinated, not duplicative, and together serve current and future residents across North Kitsap.

 

The PERC project is a two adult, tournament-sized soccer field project in partnership with Olympic College. While we are excited to see this project get to the construction phase in 2026, it will satisfy less than one quarter of today’s needs and shortfall – and a fraction of tomorrow’s future demand. 

 

Why would a sports & recreation campus be good for North Kitsap?
A sports and recreation complex would expand access to much-needed facilities for community use and youth sports while creating a welcoming space for people of all ages to gather, play, and connect.

In addition, a campus like this can support the local economy by enabling North Kitsap to host games, tournaments, camps, and retreats—bringing visitors who support local businesses, restaurants, and lodging. Communities across Washington have seen similar facilities strengthen both quality of life and economic vitality, and this project offers the same opportunity for North Kitsap.

 

In fact, an early estimate and research conducted by Kitsap Economic Development Alliance (KEDA) found this project could result in a $100M+ economic benefit to Kitsap County. A positive in our eyes as our County officials face an $8M+ budget deficit (as of January 2026) and the State of Washington an over $5B deficit (as of March 2026). 

 

Why not build closer to Kingston and within the Urban Growth Area (UGA)?
Before exploring this location, the Kingston–North Kitsap Rotary Club and its members actively evaluated multiple sites within and near the Kingston Urban Growth Area. While several properties were considered over the past 8+ years, each presented significant limitations or challenges that made them impractical for a project of this scale. Critical areas, streams, wetlands, topography logistics and county setback requirements all took possible opportunities and turned them into unrealistic project solutions.

 

The current location we are looking at represents the most viable opportunity to create a centrally-located facility that can realistically meet the needs of the broader North Kitsap community - now and in the future. It allows for the space, access, and long-term flexibility required for a community sports and recreation complex that serves multiple communities now and into the future.

 

Is this project consistent with the Growth Management Act (GMA) and Kitsap County’s Rural Wooded zoning?
Yes. This project is consistent with the Growth Management Act (GMA) and Kitsap County’s existing land-use regulations. Sports and recreation facilities are permitted outside of Urban Growth Areas under current Kitsap County code and do not require a change in zoning.

 

The Rural Wooded designation allows for certain public and community-serving uses, including recreation, when designed to preserve open space and maintain rural character. A sports and recreation complex can be developed in a way that aligns with these standards by concentrating facilities in a limited footprint while retaining substantial areas of open space and animal corridors.

 

This approach supports GMA goals by balancing community needs with environmental protection, rural preservation, and long-term sustainability.

 

Does Rotary profit from this project?
No. The Kingston–North Kitsap Rotary is a non-profit service organization and does not profit from this project or any of its work. Rotary’s role – locally and globally – is to identify community needs and help bring people and resources together to address them. The need for additional youth sports and recreation facilities has been recognized in North Kitsap for decades.

 

What about access to Port Gamble Heritage Park and existing trails on this property? 
The property in question is privately-owned timberland and is adjacent to—but not part of—Port Gamble Heritage Park. While the landowner has historically allowed informal public use of some logging roads and trails, it remains private property and public access is not guaranteed.

 

Preserving trail connections and public access is an important consideration. North Kitsap United is committed to working with partners, land stewards, and environmental and trail groups to protect existing trails and maintain connections to Port Gamble Heritage Park wherever possible. The vision is for this project to complement the park by serving as an additional gateway to the region’s trail network, while helping safeguard access that could otherwise be lost over time.

 

How many fields are planned?
The exact number of fields has not yet been finalized. Although 4-baseball and 4-multi-sport fields would get our community up to a normal, average field ratio proportionate to a community of our size. The final scope will be guided by one more comprehensive needs assessment as well as our overall fundraising efforts. It is intended that the fields will be multi-purpose and able to support more than one sport.

 

Could the fields be grass instead of artificial turf?
Field surface decisions also have not yet been finalized. Kingston-North Kitsap Rotary will work with technical experts and consider environmental concerns and recommendations, maintenance requirements, community needs, and long-term costs to determine the most appropriate field materials.

 

The goal is to select surfaces that balance playability, sustainability, and responsible stewardship of resources for year-round use as well as on-going maintenance costs and concerns.

 

What about traffic, environmental concerns, and noise impacts?
North Kitsap United recognizes that traffic, environmental protection, and noise are important community considerations and have been working on this for years. A preliminary feasibility report has already evaluated key site conditions related to soils, drainage, environmental features, and access.

 

Soils and drainage: The feasibility analysis found that soils at the site are highly permeable, which is favorable for stormwater infiltration and field construction. No streams or standing wetlands were observed across most of the study area, and follow-up wet-season observations confirmed the absence of standing water or active stream flow on site.
 

Traffic: Feasibility-level traffic analysis indicates that roadway and safety improvements near Bond Road would be needed as part of a sports and recreation campus. Potential improvements could include traffic control measures such as a signal or roundabout to improve circulation and safety as use increases. North Kitsap United is in communication with Kitsap County, Kitsap Transit, and WSDOT regarding appropriate traffic mitigation and improvements.
 

As with any significant project, development would be subject to the State Environmental Policy Act (SEPA) review process prior to permitting. The SEPA checklist addresses the environmental concerns including wetland delineation and water quality, geotechnical and soil studies, traffic study, noise study, light pollution study, etc. that determine the extent of potential development and the necessary mitigation for impacts. The SEPA checklist and all studies would be completed as required prior to county permit application. Interested parties will be notified when the SEPA checklist is complete for their review and comment. Mitigation, traffic, roadway and pedestrian improvements would also be made prior to or concurrent with the opening of this complex.

 

North Kitsap United recognizes that these are major concerns for the community. Traffic improvements will be needed and would be made prior to or concurrent with the opening of this facility, and necessary environmental reviews, studies, and appropriate mitigation will also need to be completed.

 

What about public transportation access and accessibility for low-income households? Can this project connect to the Sound to Olympic Trail?
Improving access for all community members is an important pillar and consideration for this project. Kitsap County is planning a park-and-ride facility near this land that would be served by public transportation, helping expand access for families and individuals who rely on transit.

 

In addition, North Kitsap United is working with Sound to Olympic Trail (STO) advocates to explore potential connections between the complex and the planned, ADA-accessible Sound to Olympic Trail. This would allow people to safely reach the facility by foot or bicycle, reducing transportation barriers and improving accessibility for a wide range of users.

 

Will this raise my property taxes?
No. This project is not intended to raise local property taxes. Planning and capital construction are expected to be funded primarily through private donations, sponsorships, grants, and other non-tax revenue sources. As with every YMCA project ever done, they pay for and fundraise for their project on their own from their members and donors and will not be taxing our community in order to build their facility. Since this is providing facilities that would be otherwise provided by Kitsap County, NKU entire goal is and has been to be a net reduction in future County costs.

 

Long-term operations and maintenance will be handled by a future owner-operator and are being evaluated separately. The intent is to minimize any reliance on public taxation and to keep user fees affordable and accessible for the community. This process is very similar to the timeline, actions and how the Village Green Foundation and its board went about building the Village Green Community Center in Kingston from 2007 until completion in 2016. 

 

Will we have to “pay to play”?  Will a YMCA membership be required to use the sports and recreation facilities?
No. YMCA membership will not be required to use the public sports and recreation facilities envisioned as part of this project. Kingston-North Kitsap Rotary’s vision is for these fields and courts to remain open and accessible to the broader community and their respective sports clubs/organizations.

As with other public fields in the area, teams or groups may reserve fields for scheduled use, typically for a modest fee to support maintenance - like they do already today. However, casual use and community access would not require a YMCA membership or pass. The public sports and recreation campus portion would remain accessible to non-YMCA members and all North Kitsap residents.

The YMCA hosts a weekly, Friday Teen Night free to all community members. The YMCA also offers scholarships so all youth can use their facilities regardless of income. 

 

Why isn’t a YMCA building in Poulsbo instead?
Based on a market research studies conducted by the YMCA (2016 & 2023), a Poulsbo location would be too close to their existing Silverdale facility. The studies also found that a more centrally-located North Kitsap site would better serve a larger number of potential members and more effectively address current and future community needs. 

 

Currently in 2026, over 3,500-residents in North Kitsap travel to the Silverdale YMCA on a daily basis. A facility in North Kitsap would actually help reduce a large number of those commuters traveling on Bond Road and thru Poulsbo. From the YMCA’s perspective, this location allows them to reach more residents, serve a broader population, and plan responsibly for anticipated growth in North Kitsap.

 

What is the current timeline for the project?
Our current focus it to acquire property for the NKU site within the next year. After land acquisition, we expect the permitting process to take over a year before any actual field construction gets underway. Our hope is that the start of permitting, field construction and early phases for amenities like a dog park, a frisbee golf course, playground, etc. could be a reality in late 2027, early 2028.
